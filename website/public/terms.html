Estimates and Explanations

  • Estimates, not measurements. Alertness, active caffeine and bedtime caffeine are modeled from what you record: sleep, caffeine, your half-life and sleep target settings, and the time of day. Aurora doesn’t measure anything in your body. Illness, stress, medication, meals, activity and anything else you don’t record aren’t in the model. Time of day follows a generic daily curve, not your personal rhythm. Points after now assume you record no more sleep or caffeine.
  • Half-life varies. How fast caffeine leaves the body differs a lot between people and changes with things like pregnancy, medication and smoking. Your half-life setting is an assumption you choose, not a measurement of you.
  • Cutoff scenarios. A cutoff scenario uses the bedtime, level and drink you enter. A scenario and its reminder aren’t a recommendation or a safety limit.
  • Research curves. If you turn them on, Aurora can show two published research models. Each shows its source:
    • The Three-Process Sleepiness Curve (Ingre et al., PLoS ONE 2014) is a group-style Karolinska Sleepiness Scale. It isn’t your check-in or a measurement. No published, validated caffeine term exists for it, so it isn’t shown when caffeine is on record.
    • The Unified Performance Curve (Ramakrishnan et al., Sleep 2016) shows group-average reaction-test lapses, not a personal score and not sleepiness. The papers don’t define a clock starting point, so Aurora starts the curve at your first recorded sleep. That is Aurora’s assumption. The absorption rate it uses for drinks is the paper’s approximation, not a measured rate.
    • Aurora’s code was checked against the papers’ equations, not against real people’s results. Neither curve has been validated as a prediction for you.
  • Caffeine amounts. Brand amounts come from each manufacturer’s published label or website, and other amounts from USDA FoodData Central. They’re for the listed serving, and drinks change, so check the label. Log from Photo identifies a drink on your device; it never reads an amount from the photo, so check what it fills in before you save.
  • Sample Data is labeled wherever it appears and can be cleared at any time.
